# Day 20 — APB Master FSM and Verilog Study

**Date:** 1 November 2026

## What I learned

Today I completed my planned APB protocol lecture study and worked through an introductory Verilog APB master and testbench on paper.

- Studied a 32-bit address/write-data interface, transfer request, read/write direction, active-low reset and APB request signals.
- Studied the clocked state register and combinational next-state logic for IDLE, SETUP and ACCESS.
- IDLE waits for a request; SETUP advances to ACCESS; ACCESS holds while PREADY is LOW. Completion leads to SETUP for another request or IDLE otherwise.
- Studied registered outputs decoded from next_state. SETUP captures address, control and data; ACCESS asserts PENABLE and retains the request during waits.
- Studied clock generation, reset, write stimulus and delayed PREADY in a testbench.
- The handwritten stimulus uses address 0xABCD1234 and data 0xFACECAFE, delays readiness and then ends the example.

## Corrections before implementation

The original handwritten pages are study notes, not compiled or verified RTL.

- PREADY must be an input to the APB master, driven by the slave or peripheral model. The handwritten output declaration is incorrect.
- Define three distinct named state constants with consistent widths and current_state/next_state names. The handwritten parameter declarations need correction.
- Correct signal spelling, including PENABLE, and use explicit declarations and named port connections.
- The testbench heading says slave, but it instantiates an APB master and supplies the peripheral response through PREADY.
- Avoid testbench races from blocking assignments immediately after posedge clk; drive stimulus away from the DUT sampling edge or use an appropriate clocking discipline.
- A complete APB3 path also needs PRDATA capture, PSLVERR sampling at completion and a defined upstream completion/error handshake.
- Verify reset, no-wait transfers, extended ACCESS, back-to-back transfers, reads and errors before using the example in the bridge.
