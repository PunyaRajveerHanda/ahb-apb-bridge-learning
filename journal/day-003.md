## Day 3: AMBA AHB Architecture & Burst Transfers

* **System Design**: AMBA AHB connects multiple masters via a central MUX scheme rather than PCB-style tristate buses, lowering power and enhancing performance. A central arbiter manages master access, while a decoder routes slave responses and read data.
* **Pipelined Transfers**: Implements pipelining where the address phase of a transfer runs concurrently with the data phase of the prior transfer, eliminating idle cycles and maximizing throughput.
* **Burst Operations**: Enables continuous data transfers from an initial address to minimize overhead. Modes include SINGLE, undefined INCR, fixed incrementing (INCR4/8/16), and wrapping bursts (WRAP4/8/16).
* **WRAP4 Mechanics**: For a 4-beat word burst (4 × 4 = 16 bytes), boundaries align to 16-byte blocks. Starting at 0x38 (block range 0x30–0x3F), the address sequence increments and wraps: 0x38 → 0x3C → 0x30 → 0x34.
* **Timing & Handshaking**: Address phases drive HTRANS (NONSEQ for the initial beat, SEQ for subsequent beats) and HADDR. Data lags address by one clock cycle. If the slave drives HREADY = 0, wait states insert to stretch the current data phase until ready.
